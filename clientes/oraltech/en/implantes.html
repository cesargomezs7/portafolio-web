What it is

A screw that works as the root

When you lose a tooth, you do not just lose the part you can see: the root that held it inside the bone is gone too. The implant is a screw placed in the jawbone that takes that place, so it can anchor the artificial tooth that goes on top.

That is why an implant does not rest on the gum and does not require filing down the teeth next to it: it stands on its own, inside the bone. If you are missing a tooth and are not sure where to start, message us and tell us which tooth it is and how long ago you lost it.

How it is built

From the artificial root to the tooth you see

The implant

The screw placed in the jawbone. It does the job of the root of the tooth you lost and sits below the gum, out of sight.

The connector

The middle piece that fits onto the implant and passes through the gum. It joins the screw inside the bone to the artificial tooth.

The crown

The artificial tooth: the visible part, the one that shows when you smile and the one you chew with. It is anchored to the implant and you do not take it out to eat or to sleep.

Step by step

How implant treatment works

This is what implant treatment looks like in general terms. The exact steps and timing depend on each mouth, and are only set during the clinical evaluation.

STEP 01

Evaluation

We look at the area where the tooth is missing, the condition of your gums and the bone available, to see whether an implant is the right option.

STEP 02

Placement

The implant is placed in the jawbone, in the spot the root of the missing tooth used to occupy.

STEP 03

Healing

The bone heals around the implant until it is firm. How long that takes is not the same in every case.

STEP 04

The crown

Once the implant is firm, the crown goes on top: the artificial tooth you see and chew with again.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions about implants

A dental implant is a screw-shaped medical device placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth, working as an anchor for an artificial tooth, which is the crown. In short: the implant acts as the root, and the crown is the part you see and chew with.

The procedure is done with anesthesia, so you do not feel pain while the implant is placed. Afterward, some swelling or soreness in the area during the first few days is normal, and it is managed by following the instructions your dentist gives you.

It depends on the case. After the implant is placed, the bone needs time to heal around it and hold it firmly before the crown goes on top. That time is not the same for everyone: your dentist decides it based on the condition of your bone and gums. Message us on WhatsApp and we will explain how your case is evaluated.

Not always right away. Placing an implant requires enough bone where it will go, healthy gums and general health that allows it. Factors such as smoking or uncontrolled medical conditions also matter. Only a clinical evaluation can tell whether you are a candidate.

The crown is custom-made to match the shape and color of your natural teeth as closely as possible. And unlike a removable denture, the crown on an implant is fixed in place: you do not take it out to eat or to sleep.

With the same discipline as a natural tooth: brushing, cleaning between the teeth and regular checkups with your dentist. The gum and bone around an implant can also become inflamed and infected if plaque builds up, so daily hygiene is what protects it most over time.
