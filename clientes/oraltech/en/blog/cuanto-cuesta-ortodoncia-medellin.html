How much does orthodontic treatment cost in Medellín?

Orthodontics Published by the OralTech team 6 min read

If you're looking into orthodontics in Medellín, the first thing you want to know is what it's going to cost you. Fair enough. But any website that hands you a number without having looked in your mouth is guessing: the price of orthodontic treatment depends on your case, and two people wearing the same kind of braces can pay very different amounts.

So instead of making up a number, we'll explain what actually moves that price, what a serious treatment plan should include, and the signs that you're being overcharged. With that, you can compare quotes for real — ours included.

Option 1: conventional braces

The most classic option and, almost always, the most affordable. Metal brackets bonded to the tooth and connected by a wire. This is the treatment that corrects the position of the teeth and the jaws to restore balance in the mouth and the face, and it works for everything from mild cases to complex ones.

What should a serious treatment include?

  • An initial diagnostic workup: panoramic and cephalometric X-rays
  • A treatment plan designed for your case
  • Placing the braces
  • Monthly visits with adjustments included (18 months minimum)
  • Retainers when you finish

Red flags to watch for: being charged for every visit separately, retainers left out of the price, or no diagnostic workup handed to you before treatment starts.

Option 2: sapphire braces

Brackets made from sapphire crystal, one of the toughest materials there is. They're as effective as conventional braces, with one difference: they're clear and, unlike other esthetic braces, they don't stain or turn yellow. They cost more than conventional braces, and that difference earns its keep if you care how you look during the months of treatment.

Be honest with yourself: if how you look during treatment isn't critical for you, conventional braces do exactly the same clinical work for less. You can put the difference toward something else, like the whitening at the end.

Option 3: clear aligners

Clear, removable trays: no visible brackets, no limits on talking, eating or smiling. You take them out to eat and for the meetings that matter. It's the option for anyone who wants esthetic and functional results without it showing that they're in treatment.

What should treatment with aligners include?

  • An initial workup and records to plan the movement
  • The treatment plan explained to you before you start
  • Every set of aligners in your plan, not just the first ones
  • Regular visits included
  • Refinements at the end if there are details left to correct
  • Retainers when you finish

Red flag: being quoted a price for "the aligners" and then charged separately for every new set, every visit or the retainers. Ask from the start how many sets the price covers and what happens if your case needs more.

Why do prices vary so much from clinic to clinic?

Four main factors:

  • The orthodontist's training: a certified specialist (at least 2 extra years of postgraduate study) charges more than a general dentist who also does orthodontics.
  • Technology: clinics with an intraoral scanner, 3D imaging and digital planning software have invested more, and they charge accordingly.
  • What the price covers: some clinics bill the visits separately. Others include everything in the initial price. Read the fine print.
  • Location: the part of the city the clinic sits in also weighs on what it charges.

How to choose well

Before you decide, ask for the same things at every clinic you visit. It's the only way to compare:

  • A written quote, with everything it includes (workup, visits and retainers)
  • Who is going to do your treatment and what their training is
  • What can be expected in your specific case, and roughly how long it will take

If a clinic won't give you all three, keep looking.
